How this trade works — in plain English

Leveraged ETFs target about 2x or 3x the DAILY move of an underlying stock, ETF, index exposure, or asset. Some repeatedly fall short because of financing, swaps/futures, fees, and imperfect tracking.

The idea: short the expensive or inefficient wrapper, while owning enough of the underlying hedge to reduce the directional bet.

1. Find excess dragFirst subtract normal financing and fund costs. We care about the shortfall that remains after those ordinary costs.
2. Hedge directionFor a 2x ETF, roughly $5,000 short is paired with roughly $10,000 long. For a 3x ETF, roughly $3,333 short pairs with roughly $10,000 long.
3. Manage it like a daily-reset productThe leveraged ETF resets daily. The site shows intraday drift, but normally waits until near the close before turning that drift into a rebalance instruction.
Net Excess Edge = Observed Tracking Shortfall − Normal Financing & Fees − Borrow Cost − Trading Buffer

Two different things can happen at the same time: the leveraged ETF can underperform its daily target (good for the arb) while your short dollar exposure grows too large after a rally (a hedge-management issue). The Decision Center shows those separately.

ARB statusCompares the latest session's leveraged-ETF return with its official daily target. Positive shortfall = the wrapper underperformed its target.
HEDGE statusShows an indicative next-reset hedge using current market values. During normal intraday trading, a moderate drift is usually a "check near close" item, not an automatic trade.
BORROW statusCompares your current borrow rate with the screen's break-even borrow estimate and warns when your Fidelity borrow input is old.
